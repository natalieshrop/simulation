# Roles and Responsibilities
- Red Team (4-5): Analyze responses from roles and determine whether they were effective or not. If responses were not effective, Red Team will explain that to respective teams and will guide them to the correct response for the listed event. If response is correct they will notify the teams via announcement that the event has been resolved. 
- Secretary of Homeland Security - Responsible for leading the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) which includes 22 components (ex. CISA and Secret Service). Along with running DHS, the Secretary of Homeland Security communicates directly with the White House along with being a member of the Homeland Security Council. The top priority of the Secretary of Homeland Security is to make sure that the US is protected from threats and to lead the DHS to achieve this mission.
- CISA Analyst (2)- “Responsible for the analysis and development of the integration, testing, operations, and maintenance of systems security”; “Analyze and report organizational security posture trends”; “Analyze and report system security posture trends”; “Apply security policies to meet security objectives of the system”; “Assess adequate access controls based on principle of least privilege and need-to-know”; “Assess all the configuration management (change configuraton/release management) processes”; “Assess the effectiveness of security controls”; “Develop procedures and test fail-over for system operations transfer to an alternate site based on system availability requirements”; “Ensure all systems security operations and maintenance activities are properly documented and updated as necessary”; “Ensure cybersecurity-enabled products or other compensating security control technologies reduce identified risk to an acceptable level”; “Implement security measures to resolve vulnerabilities, mitigate risks and recommend security changes to system or system components as need”; Implement system security measures in accordance with established procedures to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and non-repudiation”; “Mitigate/correct security deficiencies identified during security/certification testing and/or recommend risk acceptance for the appropriate senior leader or authorized representative”; “Plan and recommend modifications or adjustments based on exercise results or system environment”; “Properly document all systems security implementation, operations and maintenance activity and update as necessary”; “Provides cybersecurity recommendations to leadership based on significant threats and vulnerabilities”; “Verify and update security documentation reflecting the application/system security design features”; “Verify minimum security requirements are in place for all applications”
- Secret Service Agent (2) - Provide security for the President, Vice President, and their families, as well as visiting foreign dignitaries; Investigate crimes like counterfeiting, credit card fraud, and other financial crimes; Assess potential threats to national leaders and work to prevent attacks through intelligence gathering and security measures; Plan and implement security for large-scale events, including political conventions and public appearances; Conduct surveillance and gather intelligence to prevent threats against national leaders; Respond to and manage security-related emergencies, including evaluations and securing locations during threats; Develop and implement protective strategies for the safety of high-profile individuals under Secret Service protection; Train and coordinate with other law enforcement agencies to ensure effective security measures
- Director of the FBI - Lead the FBI, overseeing its operations and setting strategic direction for the agency; Direct efforts to protect the U.S. from national security threats, including terrorism and espionage; Oversee investigations into federal crime,s including organized crime, cybercrime, and corruption; Manage the FBI’s intelligence efforts, ensuring information is shared across law enforcement and intelligence agencies; Develop policies and procedures to guide the FBI’s operations and ensure adherence to legal and ethical standards; Work closely with other federal, state, and local law enforcement agencies to address national security and criminal issues; Serve as the public face of the FBI, providing information to the public and Congress on key investigations and initiatives; Lead the FBI’s response to major incidents, crises, and national emergencies, including large-scale investigations and security threats; Manage the FBI’s resources, ensuring that personnel, funding, and technology are effectively utilized to meet agency goals
- FBI Special Agent (2) - Conduct investigations into federal offenses, including organized crime, drug trafficking, terrorism, cybercrime, and corruption; Gather intelligence, analyze data, and seize evidence in connection with investigations; Work on preventing and responding to threats related to terrorism, espionage, and other national security risks; Monitor suspects and location to gather evidence, track criminal activities, and ensure safety during operations; Present evidence, testify, and assist prosecutors during criminal trials and legal proceedings; Work with local, state, and other federal law enforcement agencies to coordinate investigations and share intelligence; Uphold confidentiality in sensitive investigations and maintain ethical standards in all activities; Participate in ongoing training and specialized operations, including SWAT, cyber investigations, and counterintelligence efforts
- Chief of Police - Lead and manage the Metropolitan Police Department, overseeing all personnel, operations, and divisions; Develop and implement strategies to reduce crime and ensure public safety throughout Washington D.C.; Build relationships with community members, promote trust, and encourage public involvement in crime prevention efforts; Establish department policies and long-term strategies aligned with the city’s public safety goals; Direct the department’s response to emergencies, major incidents, protests, and public events; Work closely with city officials, federal agencies, and other law enforcement partners to address crime and security issues; Supervise and ensure the effectiveness of criminal investigations, including high-profile and complex cases; Oversee the department’s budget, staffing, and resources to ensure operational efficiency; Promote officer training, professional development, and uphold accountability and ethical standards within the force; Serve as the public face of the MPD, communicating with media and the public about crime trends, incidents, and safety initiatives
- Police Officer (2) - Maintain a visible police presence to deter crime and provide community safety; react quickly to incidents, including crimes in progress, accidents, and other emergencies; Uphold local, state, and federal laws by issuing citations, making arrests, and ensuring public order; Investigate crimes by gathering evidence, interviewing witnesses, and writing detailed reports; Ensure the safety of individuals and property through proactive policing and rapid response; Build trust with the community through outreach, dialogue, and problem-solving partnerships; Monitor traffic, issue citations for violations, and manage traffic flow at accident scenes or public events; Provide testimony and present evidence in court proceedings related to arrests and investigations; Manage crowds and ensure safety during protests, parades, and large gatherings; Accurately complete and file reports on incidents, arrests, and daily activities
- Secretary of Defense - Serve as the principal defense policy advisor to the President; Oversee all branches of the U.S. Armed Forces and manage the Department of Defense (DoD); Develop and implement military policies, strategies, and operational plans to ensure national defense; Manage the DoD’s budget, resources, and personnel to support military readiness and capabilities; Shape and guide national security policies in coordination with other government agencies; Ensure civilian control over the military as mandated by the U.S. Constitution; Coordinate with allied nations on defense and security cooperation, joint operations, and military partnerships; Direct military responses during crises, conflicts, or wars, advising the President on military options; Monitor and evaluate ongoing military missions and ensure they align with national interests; Testify before Congress, defend defense policies and budgets, and help shape defense-related legislation
- Director of National Intelligence - Oversee and coordinate the activities of all 18 U.S. intelligence agencies, ensuring effective collaboration; Integrate intelligence from multiple agencies to provide a comprehensive view of national security threats; Develop and implement national intelligence policies and strategies to support U.S. national security objectives; Provide the President, National Security Council, and senior policymakers with accurate, timely, and relevant intelligence assessments; Identify and mitigate threats to national security, including terrorism, cyberattacks, espionage, and foreign influence operations; Ensure that intelligence activities are conducted legally, ethically, and with proper oversight, protecting civil liberties; Foster relationships with foreign intelligence agencies to share information and address global security challenges; Oversee the budget and resource allocation for the U.S. Intelligence Community, ensuring effective use of funds and capabilities; Lead intelligence efforts during national crises, providing critical insights to support decision-making; Address evolving threats, sucha s cybersecurity risks, and lead efforts to monitor and combat new forms of warfare and espionage
- National Counterterrorism Center Analyst - Analyze and assess intelligence related to terrorism threats, identifying patterns and emerging risks; Integrate and synthesize intelligence from various sources and agencies to provide a comprehensive picture of terrorist activities; Provide intelligence support to federal , state, and local law enforcement and military operations targeting terrorist groups; Coordinate with the U.S. Intelligence Community and international partners to share and gather information on terrorist threats; Monitor and track the activities, networks, and movements of terrorist organizations, including individuals, funding, and operations; Identify vulnerabilities and risks in national security related to terrorist threats, recommending actions to mitigate those risks; Prepare and present detailed reports and briefings on terrorism threats for senior policymakers, government agencies, and law enforcement; Assist in the development of counterterrorism strategies, policies, and procedures based on intelligence analysis; Respond to national or international terrorism-related crises by providing real-time intelligence and analysis to inform decision-making; Continuously monitor global and domestic terrorism trends, ensuring that intelligence is shared promptly across relevant agencies
- Secretary of Transportation - Oversee the Department of Transportation (DOT) and its various agencies responsible for national transportation systems; Develop and implement transportation policies, ensuring they align with national economic, safety, and environmental goals; Manage the development, maintenance, and modernization of the U.S. transportation infrastructure, including roads, bridges, airports, and rail systems; Set and enforce safety standards for all modes of transportation (aviation, rail, maritime, highway, and transit) to protect public health and safety; Encourage and lead efforts to modernize transportation systems, integrating new technologies such as electric vehicles, autonomous vehicles, and smart infrastructure; Work to reduce the environmental impact of transportation systems by promoting fuel efficiency, clean energy, and sustainable practices; Oversee the allocation of federal transportation funding, ensuring effective use of resources for infrastructure projects and improvements; Lead the federal response to transportation-related emergencies, such as natural disasters, accidents, or disruptions to transportation systems; Foster relationships with international partners on transportation issues, including trade, safety standards, and global infrastructure development; Provide advice and recommendations to the President and Congress on transportation matters, including budget proposals and policy changes
- Secretary of Treasury -  Oversee the Department of the Treasury and its various agencies, such as the IRS (Internal Revenue Service) and the U.S. Mint; Advise the President on economic policy, including fiscal matters, taxation, and government spending; Oversee the management of the nation’s finances, including budgeting, borrowing, and managing the national debt; Work with the Federal Reserve to help manage monetary policy and economic stability; Oversee the collection of federal taxes and ensure the integrity and efficiency of the nation’s tax system; Formulate policies and regulations to ensure the stability and security of the U.S. financial system, including overseeing financial institutions and markets; Represent the U.S. in international financial matters, working with global financial institutions (like the IMF and World Bank) and foreign governments; Supervise the production of U.S. currency and coinage through the U.S. Mint and Bureau of Engraving and Printing; Lead efforts to address economic crises, financial disruptions, and to stabilize the economy during downturns; Manage the issuance of U.S. Treasury bonds, bills, and notes to fund government operations and maintain economic stability; Communicate the Treasury’s policies, initiatives, and actions to Congress, the President, and the public
- President of the United States - Enforce federal laws, oversee the executive branch of the government, and manage federal agencies and departments; Lead and command the U.S. Armed Forces, making key military decisions and overseeing national defense; Represent the U.S. in foreign relations, negotiate treaty, and engage with foreign leaders to promote U.S. interests abroad; Influence the legislative process by proposing bills, signing or vetoing legislation, and working with Congress to shape policy; Serve as the symbolic leader and representative of the United States, embodying national unity and values; Guide the nation’s economic policies, including addressing issues like inflation, unemployment, and economic growth; Lead the nation during times of national emergencies, such as natural disasters, economic crises, or terrorist attacks; Serve as the head of their political party, setting its agenda and supporting party candidates and causes; Appoint federal judges, including Supreme Court justices, shaping the judiciary branch of the U.S. government; Address national issues and set the tone on social and moral issues, guiding public opinion on matters of ethics and national values
- Vice President of the United States - Serve as the presiding officer of the U.S. Senate, casting the tie-breaking vote when necessary; Step into the role of President if the sitting President is unable to perform their duties due to death, resignation, or incapacity; Serve as a key advisor to the President, offering counsel on various issues and policies; Represent the President and the U.S. at official functions, both domestically and internationally; Advocate for the President’s legislative priorities and help build support for key bills in Congress; Assist in matters related to national security and foreign policy, often participating in key meetings and decisions; Communicate and engage with the public, promoting the administration’s agenda and responding to issues of national concern; Lead specific national initiatives or task forces as delegated by the President, such as education, health care, or climate change initiatives
- Secretary of State - Represent the U.S. in foreign relations, managing diplomacy, negotiations, and treaties with other countries; Provide advice to the President on foreign policy, national security, and international issues; Lead the development and execution of U.S. foreign policy strategies and initiatives; Oversee the operations of the U.S. State Department, ensuing effective functioning of U.S. embassies and consulates worldwide; Foster relationships with foreign governments, international organizations, and global leaders to promote U.S. interests abroad; Manage U.S. diplomatic responses to international crises, including conflicts, disasters, or diplomatic incidents; Promote U.S. values, policies, and culture internationally, engaging with foreign publics and media; Advance U.S. economic interests globally, negotiating trade agreements and fostering international economic cooperation; Direct U.S. foreign aid programs, ensuring they align with national priorities and humanitarian goals, Lead negotiations of international treaties and agreements, ensuring U.S. compliance and effective execution
- Speaker of the House - Lead and preside over House sessions, ensuring that rules are followed and maintaining order during debates; Influence and prioritize the legislative agenda by determining which bills and issues are brought to the floor for debate and vote; Act as the leader of the majority party in the House of Representatives, guiding party strategy and policy decisions; Appoint members to key House committees, shaping the legislative process and ensuring effective committee work; Represent the House of Representatives in official matters and in interactions with the President, Senatem, and other governmental bodies; Assume the role of President of the United States if the President and Vice President are unable to fulfill their duties; Communicate the views, priorities, and positions of the House of Representatives to the public, media, and other branches of government; Play a key role in impeachment procedures, including the initiation of impeachment and potentially presiding over impeachment trials in the Senate; Coordinate with Senate leadership to negotiate and reconcile legislation between the House and Senate
